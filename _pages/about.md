I am an independent researcher working on online extremism, nihilistic violent extremism, youth online exploitation, and behavioral threat assessment. I originated the Aesthetic-to-Risk Framework of Nihilistic Violence Aesthetics and the Youth Online Exploitation-Extremism Interface. I also hold adjunct faculty appointments, including at Arizona State University.

My path into this work was not a conventional academic one. I served 23 years in the U.S. Navy, then spent years in federal civilian service, before completing both a Doctor of Human Services and a PhD in Human Services at Walden University. That sequence shapes how I approach the research. I am less interested in frameworks that are elegant than in frameworks a supervisor can actually use on a Tuesday afternoon with the caseload she actually has.

The central problem I work on is distinguishing transgressive online performance from content that is moving toward warning-behavior concern. Those two things look alike on the surface, and the resemblance is not accidental. Most of my applied interests follow from that: child protection, prevention science, youth-facing media literacy, digital risk assessment, and platform trust and safety practice.

Two commitments run through everything here. The first is open access: every framework, protocol, and study is posted with a DOI, usually before or alongside journal submission, so that practitioners who need it are not waiting eighteen months behind a paywall. The second is restraint about what these tools claim. I build descriptive and interpretive instruments. Several of them concern material where overclaiming would cause real harm, either by generating false positives against young people or by handing bad actors a map. Where a framework could be misread as a detection system or a risk score, I say so explicitly in the work itself.
